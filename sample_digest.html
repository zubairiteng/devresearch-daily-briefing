Robust Reinforcement Learning for Dynamic Treatment Regimes under Model and Measurement Uncertainty
arXiv • Score: 65/100
We develop a robust reinforcement learning (RL) framework for learning adaptive treatment policies that generalize across patients and remain reliable under errors in the measured biomarker and shifts in the disease dynamics. By modeling the patient as a hidden context, we address generalization and robustness in a unified manner through robust contextual Markov decision processes. Applying our framework to adaptive therapy for metastatic prostate cancer in a simulator calibrated to clinical data, we obtain generalizable and robust policies that substantially outperform the existing adaptive therapy method.

Dexterity from Supervised Play
arXiv • Score: 65/100
How can robots autonomously collect the contact-rich experience needed to learn dexterous manipulation? We present DSP: Dexterity from supervised play, a framework in which a robot learns a play policy from minutes of human play through behavior cloning, then deploys it autonomously to collect hours of interaction data to train a world model. During inference, the same play policy then guides model-predictive control (MPC) by proposing candidate action sequences for evaluation under the learned world model.

Stability of Quantum LDPC States under Local Pauli Noise
arXiv • Score: 80/100
Quantum low-density parity-check (qLDPC) codes have a canonical mixed state---the normalized projector onto the code space---that lies at the intersection of quantum error correction and quantum many-body physics, yet the stability of its information-theoretic structure under local noise remains poorly understood. Recent progress on mixed-state phases of matter has identified the spatial decay of conditional mutual information (CMI) $I(A:C|B)$ as a key diagnostic of such stability, analogous to the role of the spectral gap for quantum ground states. We prove that, for the normalized code-projector state of a $\mathbb{Z}_d$ qLDPC stabilizer code under local Pauli noise, the CMI across a buffer $B$ separating $A$ from $C$ decays exponentially with the width of $B$ in two complementary noise regimes independent of system size.
Relevance: Directly relevant to your quantum research agenda; highlights state-of-the-art benchmarks in qubit stability and algorithmic design.
Possible research gap / opportunity: Potential research gap: Evaluating noise resistance and error mitigation when interfacing this architecture with hybrid classical-quantum pipelines.
